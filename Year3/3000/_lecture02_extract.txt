Five key points; the first four support the fifth.
1
NOTATION
The notation is independent of the number of features
Stack the rows into

everything. With five features or 5,000, the notation is identical.
2
COEFFICIENTS
A coefficient belongs to a model, not to a column
The temperature coefficient was −168.85 alone and −114.41 alongside
business day. Read every weight as “the difference this makes, holding the
others fixed” and expect it to move when the model changes.
3
TWO SOLVERS
The choice depends on the number of features
The normal equation is exact and costs the cube of the column count.
Descent approximates, scales, and works where no closed form exists,
which is nearly everywhere else in this course.
4
THE LOSS
Squaring the error was a choice with consequences
It fits a mean rather than a median, which is why one stray point can drag
the whole line. Absolute error and Huber loss are the main alternatives, and
each is one line of scikit-learn.

the central point
A score measured on the training rows is not a valid result. Give a model enough weights and it scores

If R-squared can only rise, it cannot be used to compare models with different numbers of features. Adjusted R-squared is
the version that can go down.
 is rows and  is features. As  grows, the fraction on the right grows,
so the leftover error counts for more. A new column has to earn more than
it costs, and a useless one drives the number down.
It penalizes the number of features, not their usefulness.
Adjusted R-squared has no idea whether a column generalizes,
only how many of them there are. It is a better in-sample
comparison than R-squared. It is not a substitute for a test set,
and nothing on this slide replaces the train, validation and test
split.
